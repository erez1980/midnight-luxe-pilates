Classes

Two ways to move. One goal.

On the mat or on the apparatus — every class is built around your body, your level and your goals.

Mat Pilates

The foundation of the method: bodyweight, breath and control. It builds deep core strength, improves posture and flexibility — and teaches the body to move well outside the class too.

  • Deep core strength
  • Better posture and flexibility
  • Groups of up to 15

Apparatus Pilates

Reformer and other Pilates apparatus, where springs add resistance and support exactly where they’re needed. Varied, challenging and gentle on the joints.

  • Resistance adapted to every body
  • Precise, controlled work
  • Small groups, by number of machines

Who is it for? Almost everyone.

  • Beginners who want to start moving safely
  • Experienced practitioners who want to refine their practice
  • People who sit for hours and feel it in their back
  • Anyone looking for a joint-friendly workout that truly strengthens
  • Athletes who want a strong core and efficient movement
  • Anyone who wants to leave a workout calmer than they came in

The method

Six principles. One body.

Joseph Pilates built his method on six simple principles. They guide every class I teach — from the first breath to the final exercise.

01

Breath

Breath leads the movement and connects body and mind.

02

Centering

Every movement starts from the core — the body’s “powerhouse”.

03

Control

Nothing is accidental. Every exercise is done with full control.

04

Precision

Fewer repetitions, done right. Quality over quantity.

05

Flow

Smooth, continuous movement from one exercise to the next.

06

Concentration

Full presence in class — real time for yourself.

What’s the difference between mat and apparatus Pilates?

On the mat you work with your body weight and small props. On the apparatus, such as the reformer, a spring system adds resistance and support — making it possible both to challenge and to assist. The two complement each other.

What is your certification?

I’m a certified mat and apparatus Pilates instructor, trained at the Martha Pilates Center: a 209-hour mat course and a 304-hour apparatus course.
